\begin{abstract}
A person holds a smartphone at arm's length and brings it to touch their nose. Using only the
phone's motion sensors, as a web page receives them at about 60\,Hz, we infer which hand held the
phone, for people, phones, grips and speeds the model has never seen, and we report a calibrated
probability or say ``not sure''. The key is a symmetry argument: the left-hand movement is close to
the mirror image of the right-hand one, and a small set of pseudoscalar features changes sign under
the mirror but not under any change of grip. We collected \R{data/n_trials} trials from
\R{data/n_people} adults on \R{data/n_ios} iPhones and \R{data/n_android} Android phones with a
5-minute web app. On people outside the training data the selected model reaches \Rci{main/acc}\,\%
accuracy (development cross-validation) and \Rci{locked/acc}\,\% on a locked test set opened once;
its probabilities have an expected calibration error of \R{calib/ece}. The predictor runs offline in
the browser at \predictorlink.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\begin{todolist}
  \item The question and the use case: one movement, by a person who is not in the training data,
        on an unknown phone, with an unknown grip and speed, under conditions the PI chooses
        without telling us.
  \item The output: a calibrated $\pright$ or ``not sure'' with its reason; a forced choice is
        always available.
  \item Why it is hard: the device frame changes with the grip; iPhones report the accelerometer
        with the opposite sign; handedness confounds the hand; browsers deliver only about 60\,Hz.
  \item Contributions (4--5 bullets): the street protocol and app; grip-invariant chiral features
        and antisymmetric models; person-wise evaluation with a locked test; a calibrated, abstaining
        predictor that runs offline in the browser and is parity-tested against Python.
  \item One-paragraph answer with generated numbers (\R{main/acc}\,\%, \R{locked/acc}\,\%).
\end{todolist}

\section{Why the hand is visible: the mirror and chirality}\label{sec:chirality}
Let $\Rmat(t)$ map device coordinates to body coordinates, with $\vf$ the specific force (the
browser's acceleration including gravity), $\va$ the acceleration and $\vomega$ the angular
velocity, all in device coordinates. Mirroring the body across its sagittal plane, $\mathbf{p}' = \mathbf{M}\mathbf{p}$,
and relabelling the device's $x$ axis, $\Rmat' = \mathbf{M}\Rmat\Smat$ with
$\mathbf{M} = \Smat = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, 1)$, gives for every grip and every movement
\begin{equation}
  \vf' = \Smat\vf, \qquad \va' = \Smat\va, \qquad \vomega' = -\Smat\vomega .
  \label{eq:mirror}
\end{equation}
Acceleration is a polar vector; angular velocity is a pseudovector and picks up $\det\Smat = -1$.
With $\ghat$ the unit vector pointing up, the yaw rate about gravity is a pseudoscalar:
\begin{equation}
  (-\Smat\vomega)\cdot(\Smat\ghat) = -\,\vomega\cdot\ghat
  \quad\text{(mirror)}, \qquad
  (\Qmat\vomega)\cdot(\Qmat\ghat) = \vomega\cdot\ghat
  \quad\text{(any grip } \Qmat \in SO(3)\text{)} .
  \label{eq:parity}
\end{equation}
Holding the phone with the screen facing away is the proper rotation
$\Qmat = \mathbf{R}_y(180^\circ) = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, -1)$, so it leaves every chiral feature
unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:T04}). An antisymmetric model, with logit
$z(\mathbf{x}) = g(\mathbf{x}) - g(m(\mathbf{x}))$, then satisfies
$\pright(m(\mathbf{x})) = 1 - \pright(\mathbf{x})$ exactly.

\begin{todolist}
  \item The mechanism: early in the reach the forearm swings toward the midline, so the phone turns
        counter-clockwise seen from above in the right hand and clockwise in the left; the wrist
        then turns it back to face the person, so the net yaw largely cancels and the evidence is in
        the time course (F10). Pilot: first-half yaw AUC 0.95 vs net yaw 0.58 (one person);
        replace with generated numbers.
  \item F08: top-view schematic with the two identities; F09: first-half yaw by hand and platform;
        F10: $\vomega\cdot\ghat$ over normalised time, left vs mirrored right.
  \item The 64-sign-pattern check in one sentence (figure F11 in Appendix~\ref{app:results}).
  \item Limits: the accelerometer mirror is approximate (the IMU sits off the centre line), and
        dominant and non-dominant arms move differently \cite{sainburg2002dominance,przybyla2012dominance}.
\end{todolist}

\section{Evaluation protocol}\label{sec:evaluation}
\begin{todolist}
  \item Every split is by person: stratified group 5-fold $\times$ 5 seeds (strata dominant hand
        $\times$ platform), leave one person out, leave one grip out (mirror pairs together), leave
        one platform, speed or variant out, and a learning curve.
  \item The locked test: about 25\,\% of people by a hash rule fixed before the data, the student
        and the pilot excluded, opened once.
  \item Controls: shuffled labels give $\approx$50\,\%; record-wise vs subject-wise gap; platform
        probe; mirror consistency; retry and abstention rates by hand.
  \item Metrics: accuracy (primary), balanced accuracy, AUC \cite{hanley1982roc}, log loss, Brier
        score \cite{brier1950verification}, ECE; per-person accuracy. Person bootstrap with
        $B \ge 2000$ \cite{saravanan2020bootstrap}; paired bootstrap for differences. F12: the
        split design.
\end{todolist}

\section{Probability output: calibration and abstention}\label{sec:probability}
$\pright$ is the probability that the movement was made with the right hand, for a new person from
the training population, with both hands equally likely beforehand, given that the attempt passed
the quality gates. If the share $\pi$ of right-hand attempts is known, $\ln(\pi/(1-\pi))$ is added
to the logit.

\begin{todolist}
  \item F16: reliability diagram, overall and per platform, before and after temperature scaling
        (10 equal-mass bins, person-bootstrap band); ECE and the Brier decomposition.
  \item F17: accuracy vs coverage, with $\tau$ marked; $\tau$ comes from the development
        out-of-fold predictions only, so its development accuracy is optimistic and labelled so
        \cite{geifman2017selective}.
  \item T12: ECE, Brier, log loss, $\tau$, coverage, accuracy on answered trials; mirror
        consistency $\lvert \pright(\mathbf{x}) + \pright(m(\mathbf{x})) - 1\rvert$.

\section{Limitations, ethics and reproducibility}\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{todolist}
  \item Limitations: the 60\,Hz browser cap; a sample of friends, mostly students, few
        left-handers; screen-away grips only synthesised; \code{implicit_v1} needs a still upright
        start; the accelerometer mirror is approximate and dominance is a confound
        \cite{papadatoupastou2020handedness}; single-trial decisions; choice trials carry
        self-reported labels; the PI's conditions are unknown.
  \item Ethics and privacy: adult volunteers who consented in the app and could stop or ask for
        deletion at any time; no ethics committee review, by the project's decision, so the data
        are not for publication without approval; what is stored (name, age, sex, dominant hand,
        usual phone hand, optional height) in Heroku Postgres (EU); the report and the deck use
        pseudonymous P-codes only; predict mode makes no network requests.
  \item Reproducibility: one-command builds; run manifests (commit, data sha256, seeds,
        versions); \code{pip freeze}; the model JSON and its hash; the golden parity fixtures;
        software \cite{pedregosa2011sklearn}.
\end{todolist}
