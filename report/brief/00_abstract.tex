\begin{abstract}
\noindent The task is to predict from a smartphone's motion sensors whether the left or the right hand brought it
from arm's length to the nose, as a calibrated probability \pright{}, for persons the model has never seen. The data
are \R{describe/primary_trials} instructed trials of \R{describe/n_persons} persons, and every evaluation leaves one
person out. As the mirror symmetry of the body predicts, the phone's early rotation about gravity changes sign with
the hand. Among \R{final/n_candidates} candidates, a pre-specified rule chose a one-dimensional convolutional network;
the nested estimate of this selection, with the candidates fixed, is an accuracy of \Rci{final/nested_accuracy}. With
its in-fold Platt calibrator its probabilities are consistent with calibration, and three attempts raise its accuracy
to \R{final/final_kthree} (exploratory). Without a start cue, where the final model has not yet run, a simpler
reference classifier shows a hand for \R{derived/m4_prod_shown_coverage} of the recordings and is right on
\R{derived/m4_prod_shown_acc} of them (forced choice \R{m4/prod_acc_forced}; exploratory).
\end{abstract}
