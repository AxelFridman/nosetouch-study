\section{Results}\label{sec:results}
These results use the approach window that the app stored during recording, with all tuning nested inside the
training persons of each LOSO fold. Every 95\,\% confidence interval (CI) is a person-cluster bootstrap
interval, from 2000 resamples of persons~\cite{saravanan2020bootstrap}, and each p-value names its family of
comparisons and its adjustment.

Four model families were built: rules and interpretable models on physics features, classical machine learning on
engineered features, time-frequency and deep sequence models, and a reference floor fixed before any model was built,
yaw8 (a mirror-augmented logistic regression on the cumulative yaw at 8 instants).

A rule fixed before the comparison chose the final model among \R{final/n_candidates} candidates, each scored as it
would ship (full report, Appendix~\fullref{app:final}). It keeps the leaders that paired tests cannot separate from
the lowest log loss, drops those that fail on a grip absent from training, on natural use, or with the screen facing
away even after a rest-pose check, and takes the one easiest to run in a browser.

The rule chose a one-dimensional convolutional network (1D CNN) on 12 gravity-frame and body-frame channels: the
final model. Rerunning the whole selection inside every outer fold, on the training persons only and with the
candidates fixed, gives the headline: a nested, selection-aware accuracy of \Rci{final/nested_accuracy} and an AUC
of \Rcib{final/nested_auc} (Table~\ref{tab:key}).

The final model's own LOSO accuracy, \Rci{final/final_accuracy}, is exploratory: it was the best of
\R{final/final_configs} configurations compared on the outer folds, then of \R{final/n_candidates} candidates, and
its optimism over the nested estimate is \Rcib{final/optimism_accuracy}~points. The results below describe this
shipped model and share its exploratory status.

The final model is \Rcib{final/final_vs_ref_acc}~points more accurate than the reference floor yaw8, which reaches
\Rci{reference/accuracy} (Holm $p$~\R{final/final_vs_ref_holm_acc} over the \R{final/paired_ref_m} comparisons with
the reference).

Accuracy varies by person: \R{final/brk_below80} of \R{describe/n_persons} persons score below
\R{final/person_threshold}, the lowest \R{final/brk_low_person}, and \R{final/brk_conf_err} of the
\R{final/brk_err} errors are confident (below 0.2 for the true hand), clustered in a few persons.

The body frame limits the cost of a grip absent from training: trained on upright grips only, the final model
changes by \Rcib{final/final_grip_d}~points on the sideways grips (Holm $p$ = \R{final/rob_m3_deep_grip_holm} over
the \R{final/fig_grip_tested} grip tests). The leaders that read device-frame channels change by
\Rb{final/rob_m2_lgbm_grip_d} to \Rb{final/rob_m2_lr_grip_d}~points (each Holm $p \le$
\R{final/rob_m2_lgbm_grip_holm} over the same \R{final/fig_grip_tested} tests), which removed them from the selection.

A phone held with the screen facing away defeats every model that reads the screen-relative frame: on synthesised
screen-away twins the final model falls to \R{final/final_flip_raw}. An exported check of the rest pose before the
movement, with an un-flip, brings it back to \R{final/final_flip_fixed}.

Deep networks are often over-confident~\cite{guo2017calibration}; with a Platt
calibrator~\cite{platt1999probabilistic} fitted inside each training fold, the final model's probabilities are
consistent with calibration (Figure~\ref{fig:FINAL_08_calibration}).

Its expected calibration error (ECE), \R{final/final_cal_ece}, is inside the range that a perfectly calibrated model
shows by chance (up to \R{final/final_cal_ece_null_hi}), with slope \Rcib{final/final_cal_slope} and intercept
\Rcib{final/final_cal_intercept} (ideal: 1 and 0).

No further remedy helps: Platt, temperature, isotonic and beta calibration, fitted inside the folds on top of it,
each raise the log loss slightly, none detectably (Holm $p$ = \R{final/cal_platt_holm} for each, over the four
remedies).

With $k = 3$ attempts of one person and hand, the accuracy of the mean \pright{} rises to \Rci{final/final_kthree}
(\R{final/agg_persons} persons with at least 5 trials of each hand).

The displayed aggregate, $\pright_k = \sigma\bigl(\sum_i z_i/\tauk\bigr)$, combines the logits $z_i$ of the
attempts with a scale \tauk{} fitted inside each fold ($\sigma$: the logistic function). It gives nearly the same
answers and is neither over- nor under-confident on average (calibration in the large only).

On the \R{final/final_nat_n} free-choice trials the right-hand recall is \R{final/final_nat_recall}
(\R{final/final_nat_credit} correct), with no detectable shortfall against the same persons' instructed trials (Holm
$p$ = \R{final/nat_m3_deep_holm} over the \R{final/n_candidates} candidates). All free-choice trials are right-hand so
far, so a constant right answer would score 100\,\% and this recall says nothing about the left hand.
