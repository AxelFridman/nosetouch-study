\begin{abstract}
\noindent A person brings a smartphone from arm's length to the nose with the left or the right hand. From the
phone's motion sensors (a web browser, about 60~Hz), this report predicts which hand as a calibrated probability
\pright{}, for persons the model has never seen. The data are \R{describe/primary_trials} instructed trials of
\R{describe/n_persons} persons, and every evaluation leaves one person out. The phone's early rotation about gravity
changes sign with the hand. A pre-specified rule chose a one-dimensional convolutional network among
\R{final/n_candidates} candidates; the nested, selection-aware estimate of this selection, with the candidates
fixed, is an accuracy of \Rci{final/nested_accuracy} (area under the ROC curve, AUC, \R{final/nested_auc}). With its
in-fold Platt calibrator the network's probabilities are consistent with calibration (expected calibration error,
ECE, \R{final/final_cal_ece}, null band up to \R{final/final_cal_ece_null_hi}; slope \Rcib{final/final_cal_slope}),
and three attempts raise its accuracy to \R{final/final_kthree} (exploratory). Without a start cue, a simpler reference classifier, not the final model, shows a hand for
\R{derived/m4_prod_shown_coverage} of the recordings and is right on \R{derived/m4_prod_shown_acc} of them (forced
choice \R{m4/prod_acc_forced}; exploratory, the best of \R{m4/n_configs_final} pipelines). The main gaps are \R{describe/n_left_handed} left-handed persons, right-hand
free-choice trials only and no cue-free run of the final model yet.
\end{abstract}
