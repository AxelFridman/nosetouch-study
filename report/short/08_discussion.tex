\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

It was possible to predict the hand that brought a phone to the nose from the phone's motion sensors alone in most
cases, for people the model had never seen: we estimate that a small neural network identifies the hand of a single
movement with an accuracy of \R{final/nested_accuracy}, and of three movements made with the same hand with about
\R{final/att_k3_used_acc} (slightly optimistic, Section~\ref{sec:results}). The cue is
physical: early in the movement the phone turns about the vertical in opposite directions for the two hands. We also
explored ways to make the prediction more stable and valid, such as checking and correcting its calibration and
combining several attempts. In one person with Parkinson's disease, several characteristics of the movement were
unusual, but this hand cue was typical (\R{x1/fhy_hand_pct}th percentile). The main limitation is the sample: only \R{describe/n_persons}
participants, all students aged \R{describe/age_min} to \R{describe/age_max}, of whom \R{describe/n_left_handed} are
left-handed and \R{describe/persons_ios} used an iPhone, so the accuracy may differ for older or left-handed people and
for other phones.
