\begin{abstract}
\noindent A person holds a smartphone at arm's length and brings it to the nose with the left or the right hand.
This report predicts which hand did it from the phone's motion sensors (a web browser, about 60~Hz), as a
calibrated probability \pright{}, for persons the model has never seen. The data are \R{describe/primary_trials}
instructed trials of \R{describe/n_persons} persons; every evaluation leaves one person out. The mirror symmetry of
the body explains the signal: the phone's early rotation about gravity changes sign with the hand. Among
\R{final/n_candidates} candidates, a pre-specified rule chose a one-dimensional convolutional network on
gravity-frame and grip-independent body-frame channels. The nested estimate of this selection, with the candidates
fixed, is an accuracy of \Rci{final/nested_accuracy} (area under the ROC curve \R{final/nested_auc}); the shipped
model's own numbers are exploratory. With its in-fold Platt calibrator, its probabilities are consistent with
calibration (expected calibration error \R{final/final_cal_ece}, null band up to \R{final/final_cal_ece_null_hi};
slope \Rcib{final/final_cal_slope}; intercept \Rcib{final/final_cal_intercept}). Three attempts raise the accuracy
to \R{final/final_kthree}. Without a start cue, a simpler reference classifier changes by
\Rb{m4/d_prod_vs_oracle}~points (exploratory, the best of \R{m4/n_configs_final} pipelines): it shows a hand for
\R{derived/m4_prod_shown_coverage} of the recordings, right on \R{derived/m4_prod_shown_acc} of them
(\R{m4/prod_acc_forced} forced-choice). The main gaps: two left-handed persons, right-hand free-choice trials only,
and a production simulation without the final model.
\end{abstract}
