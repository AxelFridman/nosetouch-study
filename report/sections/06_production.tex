\section{Towards production: cue-free detection and stopping}\label{sec:production}

In production there is no start cue and no stored bound. The page receives a continuous stream; it must find the
approach, reject other movements, decide when to answer and show a calibrated \pright{}. A simulation of this
pipeline ran on the full recordings (Appendix~\ref{app:prod}). Its numbers use the simulation's own reference hand
classifier, yaw8 (a mirror-augmented logistic regression on the cumulative yaw at 8 instants), \emph{not} the final
CNN. They measure what detection and stopping cost, not how the final model performs without a cue; running the
final model inside this simulation is the next step (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{Finding the approach}\label{sec:production:detection}
Figure~\ref{fig:M4_01_detection_outcomes} shows what each detector hands to the classifier. The baseline detector,
v0, takes the first movement after a fixed stillness: its first detection is the approach in
\R{describe/cuefree_share_approach} of recordings, part of the approach in
\R{describe/cuefree_share_approach_partial}, an earlier movement in \R{describe/cuefree_share_earlier_movement},
the return in \R{describe/cuefree_share_return} and nothing in \R{describe/cuefree_share_none}. The production detector cuts the stream into candidate movement
segments and scores each with a segment classifier that does not use the hand, from features such as the stillness
before the segment, its displacement and the change of the up vector. It accepts the most likely candidate when
its probability of being an approach is at least \R{m4/det_threshold}. Its scored segment is the approach in
\R{m4/prod_share_approach} of recordings, part of the approach in \R{m4/prod_share_approach_partial}, an earlier
movement in \R{m4/prod_share_earlier_movement} and the return in \R{m4/prod_share_return}; it rejects
\R{m4/prod_share_rejected} (Appendix~\ref{app:prod:detector}).

\subsection{What detection costs}\label{sec:production:ladder}
Figure~\ref{fig:M4_02_accuracy_ladder} gives the accuracy ladder of the yaw8 classifier: \Rci{m4/oracle_acc} on the
oracle windows, \Rci{m4/vzero_acc} behind v0 and \Rci{m4/prod_acc} in the production pipeline, where an abstention
earns half credit. An abstention is a rejected recording or a calibrated \pright{} of exactly 0.5. The production
pipeline accepts an approach in \R{m4/prod_coverage} of recordings, but \R{m4/prod_n_answered_tie} of these
\R{m4/prod_n_answered} get exactly 0.5 from the calibrator (Section~\ref{sec:production:calibration}). It therefore
shows a hand for \R{derived/m4_prod_shown_coverage} of recordings (its coverage), is right on
\R{derived/m4_prod_shown_acc} of those, and scores \R{m4/prod_acc_forced} when forced to answer every recording.
Detection changes the accuracy by \Rcib{m4/d_prod_vs_oracle}~points against the oracle windows (Holm $p$ =
\R{m4/d_prod_vs_oracle_p_holm}, family of \R{m4/paired_family_n} comparisons). The pipeline was chosen among
\R{m4/n_configs_final} cue-free pipelines on the outer folds, so its number is exploratory.

The detector relies on the stillness the app enforced before each trial. When the stillness before the approach is
cut, the production pipeline falls from \R{m4/stress_production_1_acc} with 1~s of stillness kept to
\R{m4/stress_production_0_acc} with none, when it answers only \R{m4/stress_production_0_cov} of recordings
(Appendix~\ref{app:prod:stress}). A model-free check of the rest pose catches a phone held with the screen away:
on synthesised screen-away twins it stops \R{m4/stressflip_production_rec_gate} of recordings and cuts the share
answered wrongly from \R{m4/gripwrongflip_rec_without} to \R{m4/gripwrongflip_rec_with} (Holm $p$~\R{m4/gripwrongflip_rec_p_holm}, family
of \R{m4/grip_family_n}), while it stops \R{m4/stress_production_rec_gate} of the recordings held toward the person
(Appendix~\ref{app:prod:grip}).

\subsection{Online logic and the stopping rule}\label{sec:production:stopping}
Multi-attempt sessions were synthesised by splicing a person's cued recordings with 1~s of stillness at each
junction, and the pipeline was replayed on them sample by sample (Figure~\ref{fig:M4_08_timeline}). With up to three
accepted attempts the session accuracy is \Rci{m4/sess_1_k3_acc}, against \Rci{m4/sesstrue_1_k3_acc} when only the
true approaches are kept. Only \R{m4/sessdet_precision} of the accepted attempts are approaches; the others, earlier
movements and returns, cancel most of the gain of combining (Appendix~\ref{app:prod:sessions}). The stopping rule
answers once the aggregated \pright{} leaves the band between $1 - p_{\mathrm{stop}}$ and $p_{\mathrm{stop}} =
\R{m4/p_stop}$, or after \R{m4/k_max} accepted attempts. It reaches \Rci{m4/stop_1_acc} after
\R{m4/stop_1_attempts} accepted attempts on average, and it answers \R{m4/stop_1_coverage} of the sessions. Its
aggregation scale is fitted in each fold on the training persons' sessions, but this scale was added after the
held-out sessions had shown the problem, so the rule is post hoc and exploratory (\R{m4/n_configs_sessions}
configurations; Appendix~\ref{app:prod:stopping}).

\subsection{The calibrated output}\label{sec:production:calibration}
For one touch, a calibrator chosen inside each fold among antisymmetric ones keeps the mirror property, P(mirror)
$= 1 - P$; symmetrised isotonic regression won in \R{m4/prod_calibrator_isotonic_sym_folds} of \R{m4/prod_folds}
folds. The calibrated one-touch output has ECE \R{m4/cal_nested_ece} (null band \R{m4/cal_nested_ece_null_lo} to
\R{m4/cal_nested_ece_null_hi}), slope \Rcib{m4/cal_nested_slope} and intercept \Rcib{m4/cal_nested_intercept},
consistent with calibration (Appendix~\ref{app:prod:calibration}). At the stop, the session-fitted scale gives ECE
\R{m4/stopcal_scale_sessions_ece}, at the upper edge of its null band (up to
\R{m4/stopcal_scale_sessions_ece_null_hi}), slope \Rcib{m4/stopcal_scale_sessions_slope} and intercept
\R{m4/stopcal_scale_sessions_intercept}, whose CI was not saved: a borderline result that needs a prospective test.

\subsection{Natural use: the main failure mode}\label{sec:production:natural}
The detector fails more often on free choice (Table~\ref{tab:m4_natural}). It rejects \R{m4/nat_rejected} of
\R{m4/nat_n} natural-use recordings (\R{m4/nat_rejection_rate}, against \R{m4/prim_rejection_rate} on the primary
set; \R{m4/nat_expected_rejections} rejections expected from each person's own rate, Poisson-binomial
$p$ = \R{m4/nat_p_poisson}, unadjusted, a single pre-planned test). The production pipeline accepts an approach in
\R{m4/prod_nat_answered} of them, \R{m4/prod_nat_answered_tie} of which gets exactly 0.5, so it shows a hand for
\R{derived/m4_prod_nat_n_shown} (\R{derived/m4_prod_nat_shown_coverage}). Its right-hand recall is
\R{derived/m4_prod_nat_shown_acc} on the trials with a hand shown, \R{m4/prod_nat_acc_forced} forced-choice and
\Rci{m4/prod_nat_acc} with half credit. All
these trials are right-hand trials, so a constant right answer would score 100\,\%. Free-choice approaches look
less like the instructed approaches the detector learned from, and the detector must learn them before production
(Appendix~\ref{app:prod:natural}).

\subsection{Instructions for the user}\label{sec:production:instructions}
Three instructions follow from these results: hold the phone with the screen facing you, tilted slightly up; keep
still for 1~s before the movement; and make several attempts. The decision comes a median \R{m4/lat_touch_ms}~ms
after the first screen touch (Appendix~\ref{app:prod:abstain}).

\subsection{The exported final model}\label{sec:production:export}
The final model is exported for the inference page as a numpy-only reference implementation
(Appendix~\ref{app:final:export}): \R{final/export_params} fitted numbers in \R{final/export_kb}~kB of JSON, about
\R{final/export_ms}~ms per prediction. It reproduces the fitted pipeline to \R{final/export_maxdp} on all
\R{final/export_rows} trials. The export carries the rest-pose check and the scale \tauk{} of
equation~\eqref{eq:aggregate}. It has not yet run behind the cue-free detector.
