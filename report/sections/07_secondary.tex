\section{Secondary analyses: speed, grip, handedness and demographics}\label{sec:secondary}

The same recordings carry more than the hand. These secondary models feed nothing that the page displays, so the
report makes no calibration claim for them. All use LOSO with nested tuning and balanced accuracy, the mean recall
over the classes, because the classes are unbalanced (Appendix~\ref{app:sec:design}).

\paragraph{Instructed speed.} The app ended a slow trial with stricter criteria than the other trials, so the
oracle windows carry the speed label in their length. Re-running the app's detector with one parameter set for
every trial gives label-blind windows (Appendix~\ref{app:sec:segmentation}). On these, the pre-specified
multinomial LR on 16 mirror-invariant kinematic features reaches a balanced accuracy of
\Rci{sec/speed_lr_common_bacc} (chance \R{sec/chance_speed}; Figure~\ref{fig:SEC_02_speed_confusion}). On the
oracle windows it reaches \Rci{sec/speed_lr_bacc}, a label-informed and optimistic figure (paired difference
\Rcib{sec/pair_speed_lr_common_vs_speed_lr_d_bacc}~points, Holm $p$~\R{sec/pair_speed_lr_common_vs_speed_lr_d_bacc_p_holm} over the balanced-accuracy
comparisons of Appendix~\ref{app:sec:speed-models}). LightGBM is not better (difference
\Rcib{sec/pair_speed_gbm_common_vs_speed_lr_common_d_bacc}~points, Holm $p$ =
\R{sec/pair_speed_gbm_common_vs_speed_lr_common_d_bacc_p_holm}). Two caveats limit the result. The protocol
confounds speed with grip: the grip alone predicts the instructed speed at \Rci{sec/speed_grip_bacc}, and the
headline does not beat it in balanced accuracy (Holm $p$ =
\R{sec/pair_speed_lr_common_vs_speed_grip_d_bacc_p_holm}). And the fast instruction was weak: it raised the peak
angular speed for \R{sec/manip_standard_peak_natural_fast_n_expected} of
\R{sec/manip_standard_peak_natural_fast_n} persons (Holm $p$~\R{sec/manip_standard_peak_natural_fast_p_holm} over the manipulation contrasts), but its effect on the duration is uncertain
(natural/fast duration ratio \Rcib{sec/manip_standard_dur_fast_natural_ratio}, Holm $p$ =
\R{sec/manip_standard_dur_fast_natural_p_holm}; Appendix~\ref{app:sec:manipulation}).

\paragraph{Grip.} The grip is recognised at a balanced accuracy of \Rci{sec/grip_lr_bacc} from the up vector in
device coordinates while the phone rests before the movement; a 12-feature model gives identical predictions. The
\R{sec/grip_lr_n_errors} errors come from \R{sec/grip_lr_n_persons_with_errors} persons, and all
\R{sec/grip_lr_n_up_matches_predicted} lie in the up-vector cluster of the predicted grip. These trials were
probably held in another grip than instructed, although no independent record confirms it
(Appendix~\ref{app:sec:grip}).

\paragraph{Dominant hand.} Mirror-invariant kinematics show no detectable dominant-hand signal
(Figure~\ref{fig:SEC_06_dominance_auc}): the pooled AUC is \Rci{sec/dom_auc}, against a within-person permutation
null with mean \R{sec/dom_null_pooled_mean} ($p$ = \R{sec/dom_p_pooled}, unadjusted, a single pre-specified test). With only \R{sec/dom_n_left} left-handed
persons, no person-level handedness model was fitted; they are shown individually
(Appendix~\ref{app:sec:dominance}).

\paragraph{Demographics.} Production has no demographics. A quick, unaudited first-pass test asked whether they
would help if it had (Appendix~\ref{app:sec:demo}). A second-stage logistic regression on the final model's logit,
fitted inside each fold, was given the persons' age, sex, height, handedness and usual phone hand. Against the same
second stage without covariates, adding them changes the accuracy by \Rcib{demo/main_dacc}~points (Holm $p$ = \R{demo/main_holm_acc} over the two add-on
variants), and adding their
interactions with the logit raises the log loss by \Rcib{demo/inter_dll} (Holm $p$ = \R{demo/inter_holm_ll};
Figure~\ref{fig:DEMO_01_paired} in Appendix~\ref{app:sec:demo}). The base model is the shipped final model, whose selection is exploratory.
Demographics are not added. Natural-use recall cannot judge such an add-on, because a rule that always answers right
scores \R{demo/ruleright_nat_recall} on the all-right free-choice trials.
